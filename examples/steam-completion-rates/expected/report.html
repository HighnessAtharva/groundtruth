Nobody Finishes These

A wishlist is a promise to your future self. A completion percentage is what your future self actually did. The gap between the two is enormous, and it is not explained by the thing everybody assumes explains it.

Twelve titles, paired with their achievement tables. Length barely predicts whether people finish. The first hour does.

Length is not the answer

Only 4.1 percent of owners earned the final achievement in Ashfall Reach, a 31-hour game. That is the number people expect, and they explain it with the 31 hours.

Then look at The Quiet Fathom. It runs 28 hours and 24.6 percent of owners finished it, six times the rate of a game two hours longer. And Copperhead Junction is five and a half hours long with a 7.8 percent finish rate, worse than either of the long ones.

Games under six hours finish at roughly triple the rate of games over thirty. That is a real effect and it is also the smallest of the three effects in this data.

The first hour is the answer

The interesting column is not the last achievement. It is the second one.

Nine Lanterns puts 88.1 percent of owners past the second achievement and 52.3 percent through the whole game. Ashfall Reach puts 92.1 percent past the first achievement and only 44 percent past the second, and the note on that row says its systems tutorial runs 90 minutes.

That is the shape everywhere in this sample. Almost everybody earns the first achievement, because it is handed to you. What separates the games people finish from the games people abandon is whether the second one arrives before the patience runs out.

Harrow Line has no tutorial and its first fight starts at four minutes.

What the pattern predicts

The share of owners who earn the second achievement is the strongest single predictor of completion in this sample. If you are shipping something, the actionable version is blunt: whatever your second achievement is, move it earlier.

Completion falls off a cliff past the ten hour mark.

What this does not show

Twelve titles is not a sample, it is an anecdote with a spreadsheet attached. Every number here is invented for the purpose of demonstrating a verification tool, which is a fact stated in the frontmatter, in the provenance file, and now here.

Players abandon most games in the first twenty minutes.
